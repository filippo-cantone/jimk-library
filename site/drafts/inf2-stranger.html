The Stranger: A Whole-Unit Inference

Book lessonReading · InferringThe Stranger · Chris Van Allsburg
One book, one driving question — Who was the stranger? Students collect odd details from the illustrations and the text until the final inference clicks.

You will need the picture book The Stranger by Chris Van Allsburg (school library) — read the full text here.

Steps: before → during → after

  1. 1Before reading — based on a picture walk the students infer the time of year in which the story takes place. Record Inferences from Pictures / Evidence from Pictures; confirm predictions when the text is read.
  2. 2During reading — students use Reading Response Journals during Read Aloud and note any unusual things that they hear. Pauses during reading can be used to formulate questions.
  3. 3After reading — "Who was the stranger?" Students use clues from the illustrations and the text to infer the stranger's identity, using the organiser: Questions in my head / Evidence from illustrations / Evidence from the text / My conclusion.

Worked example: clues that don't add up

It was the time of year Farmer Bailey liked best, when summer turned to fall… "Oh no!" he thought. "I've hit a deer." But it wasn't a deer. The doctor says of his thermometer: "Oh, you can throw that out. It's broken, the mercury is stuck at the bottom." Final clue, etched in frost on the farmhouse windows: "See you next fall."

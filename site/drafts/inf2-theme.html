Inferring Theme: Fables to Teammates

LessonReading · InferringFables · Teammates
Themes are rarely written out — readers infer the lesson using background knowledge, the pictures and the words. Fables are written to teach a lesson, so they are the training ground.

Steps: model → guided → independent

  1. 1Model — think aloud as you read about the personal connections you are making. Make explicit how you felt, and show how to use that knowledge plus the text to determine the lesson the author is trying to teach us.
  2. 2Guided practice — read the text aloud again and ask students if they learnt a lesson from the fable. Students explain how they used their prior knowledge and textual clues to draw a conclusion.
  3. 3Independent practice — When you go to Independent Reading today, think about the story you are reading. Try to notice if you are left thinking about a message or lesson the author has sent you. Track thinking with post-its, a Reading Response Journal or a 2-column chart. Follow up in Reading Conferences.

Worked example: The Hippopotamus at Dinner (Fables, Arnold Lobel)

"I will have the bean soup, the Brussels sprouts, and the mashed potatoes. Please hurry, I am enormously hungry tonight!" … "Perhaps I should not have eaten quite so many Brussels sprouts," he said, as he gazed into the gloom of the darkened restaurant. The stated lesson: Too much of anything, often leaves one with a feeling of regret. Next, try Teammates: anchor-chart the inferred themes — anger, racial inequality, self-control — then turn and talk about the big ideas (bravery, fairness, friendship, taking a stand).

Reciprocal Reading: 4 Strategies

RoutineReading · InferringSmall group · Short chunks
Four strategies students practise on small chunks of text — predicting, clarifying, question generating, summarising. Model each one, then fade your involvement as soon as possible.

Steps: the four strategies

  1. 1Predicting — sets up purpose for the reading. The student is anticipating what will come next, based on appropriate prior knowledge, and on the structure and content of the text. As students read, predictions are confirmed or modified.
  2. 2Clarifying — uses context and word knowledge to overcome difficulties: unfamiliar vocabulary; text structured in an unfamiliar way; new or difficult concepts; when they lose track of the meaning.
  3. 3Question Generating — identify the kind of information that provides the substance for an appropriate question, then frame that question (before, during, and after reading). Students posing and answering their own questions get much more involved than merely responding to the teacher's questions.
  4. 4Summarising — the student is identifying and integrating important information: initially identify the most important content of the section, then integrate and own the information.

Worked example: when meaning breaks down

A group loses track of the meaning, so they run the Clarifying fixes in order: Rereading. Using the context of the passage. Using their knowledge of written language — e.g. vocabulary, structure, grammar. Using a dictionary or thesaurus to check words. It may be necessary to read the whole passage again, to understand the meaning.

Printable: implementation checklist

Teachers should: be familiar with text; provide a brief focused introduction; link the text to current content area where appropriate; model the strategies and support students in using them; regularly monitor students' use of strategies; reduce their own involvement in the dialogue as soon as possible. During the group lesson, students discuss the text only in small chunks.

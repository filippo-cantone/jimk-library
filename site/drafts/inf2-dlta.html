DLTA + Granddaddy's Gift

RoutineListening · InferringGranddaddy's Gift · Whole class
The Directed Listening–Thinking Activity uses prior knowledge to lift listening comprehension: predict from the cover, read in chunks, and confirm or change predictions at 5–6 stopping points.

Steps: DLTA procedure

  1. 1Prepare — select five or six stopping points in the Read Aloud prior to lesson (not too many). Material: predictable story where there is a problem, sequence of events leading to a conclusion.
  2. 2Predict — predict from title/front cover.
  3. 3Read — read to first stopping point.
  4. 4Confirm or modify — at each stopping point the students either confirm prediction or modify.
  5. 5Repeat — prediction/confirmation-modification cycle continues through story.

Worked example: Granddaddy's Gift lesson

Little Joe loves her granddaddy so much she would rather stay home with him than go to school. But Granddaddy tells her she needs to go to school so she will have choices when she grows up. Before reading, ask: What do you think this story will be about? Why do you think so? What do you think Granddaddy's gift will be? Why do you think that? To whom will he give the gift? During reading, run the Discussion Web on: Little Joe's granddaddy should have to take a test to be able to vote — groups of four argue both sides, then form a group consensus.

Printable: after-reading check (print for students)

After reading: students compare the ending of the story with their predictions. Discuss: What was the gift that Granddaddy gave? To whom did Granddaddy give the gift? Why should people vote? Why is it important to vote?

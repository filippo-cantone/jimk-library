IMAGES
“When readers create mental images, they engage with texts in ways that
make it personal and memorable to them alone. Anchored in prior knowledge,
images come from the emotions and ail five senses, enhancing understanding,
and immersing the reader in rich detail."
- Keene & Zimmerman
Poetry bridges whole group work and independence. It is short, thought-
provoking, and full of images.
(Refer to Visualising in Reading Strategies handout)
Through explicit lessons children explore how
- Images are created from reader’s background knowledge and
words in the text.
- Readers create images to form unique interpretations, clarify
thinking, draw conclusions, and enhance understanding.
- Readers’ images are influenced by the shared images of others.
- Images change; readers adapt them to incorporate new information
as they read.
- Evoking vivid mental images helps readers create vivid images in
their writing.
Sample lesson.
Images are created from readers’ background knowledge and words in
the text.
10 copies of 3 or 4 poems - e.g. from last year's students.
Read each poem aloud several times.
“Listen carefully and think about which poem creates the most vivid mental
images for you.”
Students take a copy of a poem, read it themselves, think about images and
draw. Children who choose the same poem share images in groups, then
class,
Images are pictures and the feelings that accompany them. In writing we
create these pictures with words.
Images can be created through a description of
- Action
- Setting
- Dialogue

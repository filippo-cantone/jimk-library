DEBATING
Debating is an organised form of argument that involves looking at
issues and justifying a point of view.
Through debating students will construct and defend logical
arguments. Oral language is enhanced when they effectively
address an audience. I.e. students learn how to:

© Think purposefully

e Speak persuasively

e Argue a point of view

e Defend that view

e Work cooperatively in a team

In order to support their point of view students will also be
engaged in purposeful research, expanding general knowledge, and
developing comprehension skills. Like the written genres of
argument and discussion, the students will be using the
Information Process.

e@ What do I really want to find out?

e Where can I find the information I need?
e What information do T really need to use?
e How can I best use this information?

e How can I present it?

e What did I learn from this?

Debating need not be competitive, particularly in the early
years. All children in the class can be involved in helping to gather
information, suggesting arguments, writing and delivering speeches,
and listening purposefully.

Role Play Debate

Students assume the roles of various stakeholders in a debate,
Students examine different points of views or perspectives related tio
an issue,

E.g. Should students be required to wear uniforms to school?
Stakeholders - opinions expressed by, students, parents, principal,
teacher, clothing store proprietor, etc.

Prepare index card with names of stakeholders (three for each).
Students randomly draw an index card. Students with same stakeholder
card get together. These groups are responsible for becoming the
stakeholder and putting forward an argument they would forward ina
debate.

When it comes time to debate, each stakeholder presents his/her point
of view. After the presentations, the entire class can join in by asking
questions of the individual stakeholders. The students can decide which
side of the debate presented the strongest case.

Extension.

Present a number of questions for debate. Students decide which
stakeholders should be represented in a debate of each question. Then
each student is assigned a question. Students assigned the same question
gather together. Each decides which stakeholder they will represent.
Students research the question from the perspective of their assigned
stakeholder. Each small group presents their debate to the class.

Puss in Boots: When is Trickery Justified?
In this French fairytale a clever cat engineers a succession of hoaxes and
lies for the benefit of his master. As a result, his master eventually
marries the king's daughter and appoints Puss in Boots prime minister,
and everyone lives happily ever after.
The story begins with the introduction of a young man whose poor father
has died and left him with nothing but a cat named Puss in Boots. The cat
proves tireless in his devotion to his master, and begins by delivering a
sequence of gifts (rabbits, pheasants, and other game) to the king and
queen. Each time, Puss in Boots announces that the gifts are from “The
Marquis of Carabas." The king comes to believe the Marquis of Carabas
is a person of great consequence.

“Was Puss in Boots wrong to lie to the king and to deceive him?”

(Honesty versus loyalty and friendship).
After several clever tricks, Puss in Boots leads the king and his lovely
daughter to a castle belonging to an ogre. Running ahead of the group,
the frisky feline dares the ogre to transform himself into a mouse.
When the ogre successfully transforms himself, Puss in Boots promptly
pounces on the hapless creature and devours him. That enables his young
master, who arrives moments later with the king and his entourage, to
claim that the castle is his own, In so doing, the young man clinches his

There was a miller whose only inheritance to his three sons was his mill, his donkey, and his cat. The division was

imagine."
The cat's master did not build very much upon what he said. However, he had often seen him play a great many
cunning tricks to catch rats and mice, such as hanging by his heels, or hiding himself in the meal, and pretending to
be dead; so he did take some hope that he might give him some help in his miserable condition.
After receiving what he had asked for, the cat gallantly pulled on the boots and slung the bag about his neck.
Holding its drawstrings in his forepaws, he went to a place where there was a great abundance of rabbits, He put
some bran and greens into his bag, then stretched himself out as if he were dead. He thus waited for some young
rabbits, not yet acquainted with the deceits of the world, to come and look into his bag.
He had scarcely lain down before he had what he wanted. A rash and foolish young rabbit jumped into his bag, and
the master cat, immediately closed the strings, then took and killed him without pity.
Proud of his prey, he went with it to the palace, and asked to speak with his majesty. He was shown upstairs into the
king's apartment, and, making a low bow, said to him, "Sir, I have brought you a rabbit from my noble lord, the

Another time he went and hid himself in a grain field. He again held his bag open, and when a brace of partridges

from time to time for two or three months, to take game to his majesty from his master.
One day, when he knew for certain that the king would be taking a drive along the riverside with his daughter, the
most beautiful princess in the world, he said to his master, “If you will follow my advice your fortune is made. All
you must do is to go and bathe yourself in the river at the place I show you, then leave the rest to me."
The Marquis of Carabas did what the cat advised him to, without knowing why. While he was bathing the king
passed by, and the cat began to cry out, "Help! Help! My Lord Marquis of Carabas is going to be drowned."
At this noise the king put his head out of the coach window, and, finding it was the cat who had so often brought
him such good game, he commanded his guards to run immediately to the assistance of his lordship the Marquis of

From Tibet, this folktale is about a hunter who happens upon an elephant
pit in which a man, a lion, a mouse, a snake, and a falcon are trapped.
The lion warns the hunter not to rescue the human, saying, “I and the
other animals will prove grateful to you and will help you for your
kindness to us, so rescue them. But please leave the man in the pit, for I
warn you, he will forget your kindness and do you harm." However, the
hunter rescues all the animals and the man.
The other animals indeed later repay the kindness to the hunter, and as
the lion foretold, the man betrays him. Still, by the end of the story, the
betrayal of the man is revealed, the hunter is appointed chief hunter to
the king, and all ends well.

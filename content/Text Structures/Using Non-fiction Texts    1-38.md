Bundling
Bundling is a co-operative learning strategy that can be used in creating a
concept map.
Purpose:
e to establish a logical framework for learning or topic selection
e to define context, topics, sub-topics
e to identify links between ideas
e to organize, analyze and synthesize existing ideas
e to boost students’ confidence and participation
e to establish an effective operational model of research procedure
Procedure:
e Provide students with 8-10 pieces of paper.
e Working individually, students write words related to the research
topic (One per piece of paper).
e In-small groups students read and compare their notes, and sort them
into groups of related ideas (bundles).
° Each bundle is given a title or label ~ thus identifying sub-topics and
the relationship between them,
e Notes are arranged as a concept map under sub-headings on a large
chart.
e This may be added to during the unit.
Notes:
The ‘bundling’ process can also be used to synthesize information, after
the students have completed research.
The word lists created during ‘bundling’ can provide an introductory
vocabulary list that can be used for other language activities during the
unit.

Memory Writing
Aims
Using memory as an aid to note-taking
Reading for meaning
Rewriting information in own words
Procedure

With younger students a big book can be used for shared
reading. The aim is to have the students read the text, cover
it, remember the content, and rewrite it in their own words.
Shared writing can also be used,
Read the text aloud.
Discuss the main points and clarify meaning.
Students know that they are trying to remember as much of
the content as possible.
Cover text.
Students write text in their own words.
Compare with original. Discuss -

- material remembered

- information not included

- language used from original

- language used not in original

Note-taking/Key-wording

Aims

® helping students to be able to locate key words in a text

® encouraging the use of key words to help students rewrite the
text in their own words

Procedure

. e Select a text related to current topic. Student copies, large

copy and highlighters needed,

* Discuss key words. Model with first sentence on large text,

@ Pairs/individuals find key words in next sentence. Continue

e Demonstrate how to rewrite text using key words as guide.
Refer back to original text for meaning if necessary

© Pairs/individuals rewrite paragraphs or whole text as
appropriate.

Assessment indicators

® selects key words from an appropriate written text

® can use the key words to rewrite or paraphrase the text

© works cooperatively with partner

Paraphrasing

Aims

® providing successful opportunities to engage with texts relating
to current topics

® encouraging peer tutoring as a strategy for learning

¢ providing opportunities for speaking and listening for purposes
connected to the topic

° introducing and rehearsing key-wording as a strategy for note-
taking

® encouraging the use of key words and phrases to rewrite
information in own words

Procedure

e Select a text related to current topic

e In pairs, students take turns to read sections of the text (other
student reads along silently).

e Readers explain to their partners their interpretation of the
text

® The listener has the role of assisting the speaker/reader to
clarify ideas

@ When students are competent with oral paraphrasing, students
can write down main points such as key words, phrases and
ideas

° Students rewrite the information individually or in pairs using the
paraphrased ideas as guides to the summary.

Assessment indicators

© retells ideas from information texts in own words

e listens to, makes comments and questions ideas with a partner
when retelling or paraphrasing texts

* recognises some organisational features of information texts such
as headings, sub-headings, paragraphs

e understands that texts are constructed by people for real and
imaginary purposes

e uses strategies such as context and self-correction to establish
meaning of unknown words

® recognises and records key words, phrases and ideas in texts

e able to write summaries of texts using key words and phrases

Questions
Some question frames to encourage active learning .
Fluency: io develop the ability to produce a large number of ideas in a short period of
time. The following question frames encourage fluency:
° Who can think of the most...?
° Think of all the ways...? . :
° How many ways...?
Flexibility: to develop an ability to produce different answers. The following question
frames encourage flexibility:
° In what other ways...?
e What different kinds...?
° What else...?
Originality: to develop an ability to think of new ideas. The following question frames
encourage originality:
e Can you think of something new?
° Can you think of something completely different from....?
° Can you think of something no one else will think of?
Elaboration: to develop an ability to listen to others and develop their ideas. The
following question frames encourage originality:
° Can you add something to that?
° How can you change ...?
° Can you make this better...?
Questions to Encourage Multi-level Thinking
Literal level - knowledge: require factual knowledge to be recalled. They take the form:
® Tell me what you know about...?
. Name the ...? .
° Find the ...?
° Which ....? .
e What...?
Translation questions: require information to be changed or restructured into another
form. They may require students to draw on prior knowledge. They take the form:
° Retell the...
° Rewrite the
° Draw / make a diagram
e Tell what picture you have in your mind?
Interpretation questions: require students to discover connections between ideas, facts,
definitions and values. They take the form:
° Why / What do you think....?

<!-- page 22 -->

Application questions: require students to solve problems, or investigate. problems of
logic. They take the form:

° Solve the problem

° Show how you would....?
Analysis questions: require students to examine whether an event, process, argument is
adequately explained. They take the form:

° Compare the....and ...

° Contrast the...and....

° What is the evidence for...?

° Do the conclusions make sense?
Synthesis questions: require students to use some original thinking. They may have to
originate , integrate and combine ideas into a new idea, production, plan or proposal.
They take the form:

° Invent a new....?

° predict what would happen if...?

° compose a new...?
Evaluation questions: require students to make judgements that integrate information
into a personal value system. These questions lead students to take ownership of new
ideas and concepts. They take the form:

e Was the character right or wrong to...?

° Was a solution to a plot problem fair...?

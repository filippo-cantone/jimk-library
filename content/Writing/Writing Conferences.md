Independent Writing and Conferences
Teacher-student conferences are crucial. Through them the students
learn to interact with their own writing. Therefore the students
should do most of the talking.
Conferring is...
A conversation,
Teaching
- individual Se
- methodical
- deliberate
Content Conferences
Focus is on the subject, with the teacher responding and asking
questions about content. During this conference the teacher is not
concerned with style or technique.
Teaching peer-conferencing. (Teacher can model).
Procedure:
i. writer reads text to listener
ii. listener responds ~ ask questions if not clear
iii. listener asks questions about content
iv. writer informs listener
v. “What will you do next?"
Process Conferences
Students can explain how they went about writing the text. They can
explain methods they used in: topic choice, problem-solving, planning,
techniques employed, gathering information, revising.
“What will you do now?"
Aim is for them to reflect on their thinking processes ~
“Where do you need help?"
Evaluation Conferences
Shift onus to writer.
“How do you feel about it?"
Questions can be asked about strengths and weaknesses of work.

Key Concepts about Strategy/Technique Conferences
1 A conference is a conversation,

- Personal way we talk to students.

- Teachers talking to students, but also writers talking to writers.

2. The point of a conference is to teach students ta become better writers.

- Writers who can use the strategies/techniques that they can use on their own
later.

- Students should work towards independence with problem-solving. We can't be
with all the time when they have a difficulty. They need to use the
strategies we are teaching the class in craft lessons.

- Teaching reflection helps the students to be better writers,

We scaffold this when we ask:
“How's it going?”
“What work are you doing as a writer today?”
“What can I help you with today?”
When we're not with the students they can ask themselves:
“How's this going?”
“What do I need to do to make this work?"
“What am I trying to do here?"
We should be able to name what we did to help.
3. Writing conferences have a predictable structure.
See notes on conference structure: Research, Decide, Teach, Record.
i. Talk about the work they're working on. E.g. lead/ending, revising, editing.
With this in mind we decide what to teach.
ii. Talk about how to be better writers. We teach students to improve on the
writing work they're doing.

- following up on whole class lessons. :

i. First part of conference - the teacher asks the student to set the agenda.
“How's it going?"
“What are you doing as a writer today?”

ii, Teacher decides what to teach:
- Feedback about their writing.

TIPS FOR HAVING CONTENT CONFERENCES

« When we know we’re going to have a content
conference with a student, we might begin the
conference by asking, “What are you writing about?”

° We react enthusiastically to the student’s topic, and
to what they tell us about the topic (“Wow!” or “You
don’t say!”)

* We get kids to say more by listening intently to what
they tell us.

° We get kids to say more with prompts such as, “Tell
me more about that” or “Could you try to tell me
more so | can see what you’re talking about in my
mind?”

° We get kids to say more by saying back what we've
just heard.

« We get kids to say more by being quiet when students
pause,

¢ We resist the impulse to ask leading questions, such
as, “What color was your dog?” or “Tell me about the
day you got your dog?”

° We end a content conference by telling a student how
excited we are about her topic and saying that she
seems to have so much to say.

¢ We might get some paper out and help the student
plan what might go on each page.

Share Time
The share session at the end of the lesson is also a teaching time. In the
share time we follow up what we talked about during that day's mini-lesson.
‘The point of the share session is for students to discuss the work they did
as writers that period, not to read their pieces from beginning to end.’
C.Anderson
Students might read short extracts where they tried the craft.
One student might discuss their work done during that lesson.
Sometimes all students could participate in sharing ~ turn and talk.
‘Symphony share’ ~ every student reads one sentence from their work.
Like mini-lessons, share time help us have conferences in which students are
already doing new kinds of writing work.
Guided Writing
Where we notice a common need in a group of students, we can bring
them together for a short guided writing session.
The craft is revised.

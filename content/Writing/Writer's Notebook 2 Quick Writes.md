**Quotes**

I’m fascinated with quotes. A local shop sells them on boards, which
seems to becoming a trend in interior decorating. I usually window-shop
to read the latest:

*My husband wanted more space, so I locked him out in the backyard.*

Similarly graffiti often adds a sense of humour. My all-time favourite
was seen on a board outside a church. The board originally read:

*What would you do if Jesus Christ came to Hawthorn?*

Someone had added underneath:

*Move Peter Hudson to centre-half forward.*

**THOUGHTSHOTS**

A THOUGHTSHOT is a show-don’t-tell elaboration strategy that gives
readers a sneak peek into a character’s mind.

Lesson directions: Pretend that you are one of the characters, and for
the next 12-minutes tell us what you are thinking.

OR

*Become this character for the next 12 minutes, and WRITE A THOUGHTSHOT
that would let readers know what caused you to react the way you did
when the photo was taken.*

**Sensory Descriptions using Settings.**

Prewrite Directions:

1\) Write the title of this setting in the list section of your Writer’s
Notebook

2\) Imagine that you have been transported to this location.

3\) LIST everything you SEE, HEAR, SMELL and TASTE and FEEL while you
are there.

These lists will be used throughout the year as springboards for
narrative writing, or to revise pieces in progress.

**Literature extracts**

Use extracts from literature that have a strong emotional impact –

5-8 sentences.

*Continue with the story line from the text, or any thought you have
about it.*

*Pens/pencils must keep moving for the allotted time.*

SOS Titanic by Eve Bunting

*There was another terrible bellow as the Titanic blew off more steam.
Barry clapped his hands to his ears. He felt as if he were going mad,
like Mrs Cherry Hat. The water had moved up another step. Now it covered
his feet, icy cold through his socks and boots. How much would have to
come in before it weighed them down? Was the ship about to turn over,
belly up?*

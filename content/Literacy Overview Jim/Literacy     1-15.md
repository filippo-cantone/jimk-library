Brian Cambourne states that children have to learn how to make meaning using the same language conventions
that the rest of the community uses and that a Pedagogy has been developed which maximizes that probability. He
also states that there are certain conditions, which contribute to the learning processes which take place. The
conditions of learning are listed below.!
immersion
From the time we are born, we are saturated in the medium we are expected to learn. We hear thousands of
examples. It is important that what students are immersed in, is whole, usually meaningful and in a context which
makes sense or from which sense can be construed.
Demonstration
Demonstrations can be actions or artifacts. Young learners receive millions of demonstrations of what the spoken
form of the language means, what it can be used for, how it functions and how it can be tied to action and so on.
Demonstrations are the raw material of nearly all learning. Demonstrations can also be artifacts. A book is an
artifact. It is also a demonstration of what a book is, what print is and does, how words are spelled, and how texts
are structured. Demonstrations are necessary conditions for learning to occur.
Engagement
Before learning can occur, a process which Frank Smith labels ‘engagement’ must occur, Many demonstrations will
wash over learners and before engagement occurs learners need to be convinced:
© That the demonstration is ‘do-able’ or ‘owner-able’ by them.
@ That the demonstrations will add to the purposes of their lives.
© That attempting to emulate the demonstration will not lead to negative consequences if they fail.
Expectation ;
Learners must believe that what they are setting out to learn is actually learnable by them. Expectations can refer
to what ‘significant others’ communicate to learners. Expectations are subtle and powerful coercers of behaviour.
Responsibility
When learning to talk learner talkers are left to decide (take responsibility for) which particular convention or set
of conventions they will attend to and internalize in their repertoires. The ‘tutors’ give off strong expectations that
the task will be achieved. They provide high saturation and give meaningful demonstrations. Cambourne argues that
once we take the responsibility away from the child by deciding which layer she should or can learn, and isolating it
from others, we are decontextualising and fragmenting the language act. We begin to trivialize language.
Approximation
The willingness to accept approximations is absolutely essential to the processes that accompany language learning.
It sets in motion the “hypothesize, test, modify hypothesis, test again' cycle which characterizes all natural learning.
Without the ready acceptance of approximations the whole hypothesis/test/modification cycle will not occur.
Neither will learning.

Use
Learners need both time and opportunity to use their developing language skills. ‘They appear to need time to
practice their language skills with others, as well as time alone, away from others to practice and use what they
have been learning.
Response
This is a bit like feedback, although it is not as mechanistic. Often an exchange as a response to a child provides a
scaffold for further learning to occur. Parents usually give responses frequently with no strings attached and no
penalty for not getting the conventional form correct. These natural conditions for speech need to be simulated in
the classroom for reading and writing.

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| Read-aloud | The purpose of a Read Aloud is to help students discover meaning through listening and discussion. Reading to children stimulates their desire to become a reader. Through conversation, dialogue and discussion, the teacher assists the reader in getting meaning from, and bringing meaning to the story o[r] information text. The learners listen to complex language patterns and acquire knowledge of text structure, vocabulary and concepts from books above instructional level. It is not meant to be a stop reading of a text. The teacher needs to choose carefully where to break the text and the purpose for [doing] so. It should be enjoyable -- don't kill the enjoyment with too many questions. |
| Shared reading | Shared Reading is done as a whole class or group of learners. Students gather together to see a piece [of] enlarged text and observe and join in reading with fluency and expression. The enlarged text may be [a] book, chart or overhead transparency. The teacher models reading strategies and skills for students t[o] apply in their own reading. All work together to build meaning from the text. |
| Guided reading | The purpose of Guided Reading is to enable small groups of learners to learn and practice independen[t] reading strategies and skills successfully with increasingly difficult texts. This is based on the needs of t[he] group and is assessment driven. The role of the teacher is to introduce the text and to observe stude[nts] reading silently as they process it. Guided Reading will facilitate reading for meaning at all times. |
| Independent reading | The purpose of Independent Reading is to provide many opportunities for students to practice the reading strategies acquired in Guided Reading at a level lower than their instructional level. It is to also increas[e] reading stamina and to encourage students to become lifelong readers. During independent reading tim[e] the teacher confers with students and students may also write notes in their reading journals. |
| Reading conference | Reading conferences take place between the teacher and student during independent reading time. T[o] confer means to have a genuine conversation with the reader about how the reading is going. It enable[s the] teacher to understand each student's reading process. It is a time when the teacher can provide powe[rful,] customized instruction for the student. (Fountas & Pinnell: *Guiding Readers and Writers Grades 3-6*, p12[.]) |
| Assessment | Records from the previous year, informal Standardized Assessment records such as test results or info[rmal] 'Running Records', observations and anecdotal records can be used to gather information centered on [a] student's use of reading strategies. Records from the previous year also provide important information. This information helps the teacher drive instruction in Shared and Guided Reading and in Read-aloud. |
| Modeled writing | The teacher composes and writes the text on chart paper or transparency for the students to observe, respond and question. As the teacher writes, he or she 'thinks aloud' to help students understand the process and thinking going on. |
| Shared writing | Children work together to compose a text. Usually the teacher contributes to the text and helps guid[e the] way in which the text is constructed. The teacher has the pen or marker and does the writing so the students can focus on composing the text. |
| Interactive writing | During interactive writing, students and teachers "share the pen" at strategic points in composing and constructing the text. Teachers write those words that students know how to write as well as words [or] parts of words that are too difficult. Interactive Writing is the bridge between Shared Writing and Independent Writing. |
| Guided writing | The teacher works with a small group to provide explicit teaching based on identified needs. Sometime[s] you may use guided writing with the class whereby you guide the writers through the process of constructing a piece in a specific genre. |
| Independent writing | Must be a daily component of the writing program. Students need opportunities to choose to write fo[r a] variety of purposes and a range of audiences. They need opportunities to write about their own topic[s] for their own purposes. The students need to build up writing stamina and develop a 'writing habit'. |
| Writing conference | The structure is similar to a reading conference. Conferences should be conversational and the teache[r] should have a focus for the discussion. You try to assist the student as a writer by helping her to learn something about being a writer and not solely on improving the current piece being discussed. |

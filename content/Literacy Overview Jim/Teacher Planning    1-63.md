TEACHER PLANNING
Tn order to plan for writing in the classroom we have to know what our
expectations for the students are. What is your vision for your students as
writers?
T want students to:
> See themselves as writers. Every student comes to school ‘writing’. I
have recommended to Prep teachers that they have a writing lesson on
day one, collecting a writing sample from each student during the first
week, They then have a class profile of the stages of development.
Individual portfolios track this development. During Writers’
Workshop, the students will come to know their strengths and
weaknesses, and be aware of their own development. Just as we know
that our aim is to teach children to write well over time, the students
should understand this.
> Keep a Writer's Notebook as part of their writing life. (See Writer's
Notebook handout). The Writer's Notebook becomes a place to
record observations of the world around them; a place to have-a-go
with language. It is a place to practise writing, to find ideas; to think.
Students will realise the need to write well when they choose what to
write about.
> Build their writing stamina and fluency. In Writer's Notebook I
wrote...” We know that children learn to read by reading. The same is
true of writing, Children learn to writing by writing.
Writing..writing..writing, In reading, the tine spent each day in silent
sustained reading increases as students progress through the primary
school. They build their reading stamina and fluency. The same is true
of writing. The students need to spend this same sustained time in
writing in order to build their stamina and fluency.”
By writing for a sustained time, the students develop rigor in their
writing, constantly keeping in mind the purpose of their writing, and
planning for its development.

Techniques - ways to fashion things.
Learning the craft of writing. This is where we learn
from our mentor authors. See Craft lessons.
Craft means understanding the nature and importance of leads
and endings: of showing, not telling: of sharpening and
tightening; of structure and focus; of purpose and audience; and
of the conventions.
Mem Fox
Questions - ways to think about things.
The kinds of questions authors ask, teaches students to
ask themselves questions when they're writing. E.g.
* As I read my Writer's Notebook does a theme emerge? What
kinds of entries am I making?
Students are aware of using their Writers’ Notebooks as a tool
in many ways. One of these is deciding what to take out of the
notebook to write about.
e Revision questions.
Conventions - ways to expect things.
Often dealt with in small group guided writing or
individual conferences. Whole class lessons are on the
conventions the majority of the class need at that time.
They may include lessons on punctuation, spelling,
structure (sentence and paragraph) etc.

> **WRITING CRAFT MENTOR LIST**

Teaching the craft of writing is like teaching the reading strategies,
in that although we teach each one, they are interdependent.

“Writing teachers draw upon three distinct areas of expertise:

We must know our students.

We must know how to teach.

And we must know something about writing itself.”

-Ralph Fletcher

Begin with a few mentor texts kept permanently in the classroom. The
students will become familiar with several authors. Often the one book
can be used to demonstrate several techniques. Authors like Cynthia
Rylant, Eve Bunting, Jane Yolen, Gary Paulsen, Jerry Spinelli, William
Steig, Natalie Babbitt, Patricia Polacco, etc., etc., demonstrate a host
of elements in one passage.

The Mentor Text Library at Wembley is currently housed in the Conference
Room.

Each student has their own Mentor Text Folder. These should continue on
with the students.

The students know that they read in two ways. One is to read like a
writer, asking, ‘What can I learn from this author that will help me as
a writer?’

(See all associated Writing Courses, including Reading Like Writers.)

The mentor texts listed here are only ones that I have in my own
library. It will be updated regularly. Feedback on books to add would be
appreciated. Feel free to borrow any books at any time.

\- Jim

**Varying Examples of Organising Techniques**:

Courage - B.Waber

Uses different uses of the word courage. Students could choose another
word to follow the example – *freedom. honour, justice.*

The Z was Zapped – C.Van Allsburg

The alphabet is the organiser. Action sentences for each letter.

Switch on the Night – R.Bradbury

Sophisticated organisation allowing for different teaching approaches.
E.g. Repeated refrains.

Story map leading to resolution.

Compare and contrast beginning and ending.

The Paperboy – D.Pilkey

Transitions from dark to dawn without overt use of transition words.

Rimshots – C.Smith

Organising different genres on one theme. Students can use the model on
subjects that matter to them. E.g. other sports.

The Journey – S.Stewart

Juxtaposition between Hannah’s diary entries of city discoveries, and
pictures from her mind of life on the Amish farm.

Dear Mrs LaRue: Letters from Obedience School – M.Teague

> Letter format. Students could write a sequel in letter style.

**WRITER’S CRAFT**

**Questions to ask:**

**FOCUS:**

**Ideas:**

Do I know and write enough about the topic?

Did I stay on topic?

Is the message clear?

Are there important details?

Did I show not tell?

Did I use my five senses?

Did I use imagery to create mind pictures?

**ORGANISATION:**

Is there a reader-grabbing lead?

Is the writing in order?

Is the ending thought provoking?

Can he reader follow the text?

**STYLE:**

**Words:**

Are there strong verbs?

Are there specific nouns?

Is there surprising language?

Have I used figurative language?

**Voice:**

Does the writing sound like me?

Have I *showed* feeling?

Does it sound like I care about the topic?

Which point of view have I used?

Is this consistent?

**Fluency:**

Does the writing have a rhythm and easy flow?

Does the text sound interesting when read aloud?

Do the sentences vary in length as well as structure?

**CONVENTIONS:**

Is the grammar correct?

Is the punctuation accurate?

Is the spelling correct?

# The Whales — Cynthia Rylant

In the blackness of the Black Sea, the whales are thinking today.

Thinking of those things that matter most to them: friends, family,
supper.

A song they used to know.

The whales are thinking about where they are going and how deep are the
oceans of the world. They swim in places with beautiful names. Past Cape
Farewell, they say good-bye. At the Cape of Good Hope, they make a wish.

And under the Red Sea, their dreams are in colour.

Whales love their children, and when they are born, the babies are
gently pushed to the top of the water where they take their first breath
and see their first sky and gasp at the loveliness of living.

The Blues are humble. The largest life God ever made is a blue whale.
Yet blues are neither pushy nor boastful …. They swim past delicately
and with impeccable manners.

Each Humpback has a little song to sing, one all his own, and as he
grows older and changes, so does the song.

So many whales in the seas, swimming like birds, rolling in the
underneath sky. They are Grey and Right, Beluga and Sperm, Norwhal and
Fin. They hear everything and carry secrets between them.

But a rose is lost on them, for they haven’t any sense of smell. No
matter. They love songs and touching and can court without flowers.

O, what they have seen, swimming from one pole to the other. What they
have seen. There are not enough poems in the world to tell ….

And whales do not know, as they rise up for a big breath of air, that
someone is standing on a shore and his heart is filling up. Filling up
and ready to burst. Whales do not know how they change people, how they
make them better, how they make them kind. Like angels appearing in the
sky, whales are proof of God.

They are swimming today, in the Indian Ocean, in the Sea of Japan. The
babies are drinking their mothers’ milk and their fathers are singing
nearby. They are floating like feathers in the deep blue green. They are
floating like feathers in a sky.

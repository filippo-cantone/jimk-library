# Why We Should Bring Passenger Pigeons Back from Extinction

*Ben Novak*

The passenger pigeon is not the bird you probably imagine when you hear

the word pigeon. It’s been gone from the world for 100 years, which
means

that few living people have ever seen one flying in the sky.

The passenger pigeon lived in a way that no other bird in America lives—

in flocks made up of millions of birds. Migrating flocks darkened the
sky and

sounded like thunder as the birds flapped their wings. One reason to
bring

passenger pigeons back to life is that it would be amazing to see a
flock that

big! It would also be cool to see an extinct animal.

But it’s not just about doing something cool. Giant flocks of passenger

pigeons played a major role in shaping forests. The weight of so many
birds nesting in the trees broke many branches. This allowed extra
sunlight to reach the forest floor, which helped more plants grow.

The birds also ate huge amounts of seeds. That affected which trees grew
in the forest. If we bring back the passenger pigeon, it would play a
vital role in shaping forest ecosystems once again.

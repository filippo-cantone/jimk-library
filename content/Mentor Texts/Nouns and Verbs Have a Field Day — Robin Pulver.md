# Nouns and Verbs Have a Field Day

*Robin Pulver*

Each day in Mr Wright’s classroom, the kids searched the room for nouns
and verbs.

“Nouns are names for people, places, and things.”

Verbs are where the action is.

> Always reach for the stars.

The cow jumped over the moon.

“Who can find the most nouns? Who can find the most verbs?”

But one morning Mr Wright announced, “No time for nouns and verbs today.
It’s Field Day!”

“Hooray!” shouted the kids, and they raced outside. The door slammed
behind them, leaving the nouns and verbs all aflutter. The words felt
sentenced to a long, boring day without the kids. What’s Field Day? they
wondered.

“Look!” said a verb.

“Window!” said a noun.

The words looked out the window and saw the kids on the playground.

“Run, jump, hop, throw,” said the verbs. “Play!”

“Games, races, contests,” said the nouns.

“Listen!” said a verb.

They heard kids whooping and laughing.

“FUN!” said a noun.

The nouns and verbs wanted to play too. They wanted to have as much fun
as the kids, so they decided to have their own field day.

“Teams! Pairs!” said some nouns.

“Choose!” said a verb.

When they picked teams, the nouns chose other nouns. Desk, crayon, book,
and eraser insisted on being together. Cookies and milk paired up. So
did paper and pencil. Glue, markers, and tape stuck together.

Verbs wanted to be with other verbs. Push, pull, yank, and tow said they
belonged together. Hop, skip, and jump chose one another. Shout wanted
to be with cheer. Throw and catch refused to be separated. So did chew
and eat.

Long nouns were happiest with other long nouns. Pronouns stayed together
waiting their turn. Proper nouns made sure their team’s name started
with capital letters.

Finally the leader shouted, “Get ready, get set, go!” The nouns got
ready. But they were helpless without the verbs. The verbs got set. But
they couldn’t do anything without the nouns. The nouns and verbs stared
at one another. More than ever they felt sentenced to a long, boring day
without the kids. They were frustrated and sad and mad.

Finally they looked out the window again. Outside, Sam ran. Jacinta
hopped. Mike and Ting were throwing and catching. Kids were pulling and
tugging and falling down. Mr Wright cheered. The custodian helped.
Balloons burst. Teams were taking turns. The principal laughed. some
children waited. A breeze blew.

“We forgot …” yelled a verb.

“…our jobs!” shouted a noun.

“Things happen when we work together!”

“We need verbs!” said the nouns.

“We need nouns!” said the verbs.

Then they chose new teams and partners so they could have fun and play
their own games.

Then, “Get ready, get set, GO!” The nouns and verbs kicked off their own
field day. Nouns and verbs played tug-of-words.

“I’m a strong verb, choose me!”

“You can depend on me!”

Nouns and verbs played hide-and-seek.

Nouns and verbs played Simon Says.

Nouns chose partners for the three-legged race. Then the new compound
nouns teamed up with verbs.

Nouns acted out verbs for the others to guess. The nouns and verbs had
the time of their lives.

Finally they decided to show Mr Wright’s class how much fun nouns and
verbs could be. They made up new games for the kids. They scrambled and
juggled and twisted. They laughed themselves silly.

When Mr Wright’s class came back, the kids couldn’t believe their eyes.

> the stars reach for Always.

The over jumped moon. the cow

Mr Wright looked puzzled. “I forgot to close the window. It must have
been the breeze.”

But when Mr Wright and his kids noticed the message on the board, they
couldn’t wait to have a different kind of field day, playing around with
nouns and verbs!

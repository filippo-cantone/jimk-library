# Charlie Anderson — Barbara Abercrombie

promote understanding.
Children know that good readers ask questions before, during, and
after reading. The class may also be building a chart after their
reflection on their learning - ‘Thinking About Questioning.
In Charlie Anderson, “Charlie, a fuzzy grey cat, walked out of the
woods one evening and into Elizabeth's and Sarah's hearts. Now he
sleeps on their beds, lets them dress him up in doll clothes, and laps
up warm milk on chilly nights. But where does Charlie go during the
day?
It's not until a storm keeps Charlie away one night that the two
sisters discover his other, daytime home, But maybe that’s not such a
bad thing. Because, just like Elizabeth and Sarah, Charlie has two
houses, two beds, and two families who love him very, very much.”
Questions before reading:
Why is the book called Charlie Anderson?
Who does the cat belong to?
Questions during reading:
Why does Charlie go into the woods every morning?
Why does Charlie purr during the night?
What's the difference between a country cat and a city cat?
Where was Charlie in the storm?
When Charlie comes down the stairs, what has he been doing?
Who does Charlie love best?
What are the two houses Elizabeth and Sarah have? Why?
Questions after reading:
Does Sarah get jealous that Charlie likes Elizabeth's bed best?
Which family does Charlie like best?
During discussion, individual students can code the AT questions on
the chart. The unanswered questions are those that often delve
deeper into themes and bigger ideas, where the students have to
infer meaning.

“Charlie sleeps in my bed at night,” Elizabeth said. “He leaves for
the woods after breakfast.”
“Anderson comes home at breakfast time,” said the woman. “He
leaves right after dinner.”
They all looked at the cat. He sat at their feet, very happy and
very fat.
They call him Charlie Anderson now.
Sometimes, in bed at night, Elizabeth asks him, “Who do you love
best, Charlie Anderson?"
And she can hear him purring in the dark.
Just like Elizabeth and Sarah, Charlie has two houses, two beds,
two families who love him.
He's a lucky cat.
